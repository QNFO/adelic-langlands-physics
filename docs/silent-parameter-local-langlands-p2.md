# Silent Parameter as Local Langlands at p = 2 — Formal Identification

**Date:** 2026-07-26 | **Status:** Phase 0.4 Deliverable

---

## Statement

The isomorphism proved in ZBW Phase 2:

```
R(SU(2))_k ≅ R(ℤ₂^×)
```

is an instance of the **local Langlands correspondence for GL(1) at the prime p = 2.**

---

## The Standard Local Langlands for GL(1)

For any local field F (either ℝ, ℂ, or a finite extension of ℚ_p), the local Langlands correspondence for GL(1) is **local class field theory** — an isomorphism between:

1. **Galois side:** Continuous characters χ: W_F → ℂ^× of the Weil group W_F (or equivalently, characters of the local Galois group)
2. **Automorphic side:** Smooth characters π: F^× → ℂ^× of the multiplicative group of the local field

The correspondence is given by the **local reciprocity map:**

```
rec_F: W_F^{ab} ≅ F^×
```

which induces the bijection χ ↔ π = χ ∘ rec_F^{-1}.

For F = ℚ_p, the multiplicative group decomposes as:

```
ℚ_p^× ≅ ℤ × ℤ_p^×
```

where ℤ_p^× is the group of p-adic units. The tame part of the local Langlands correspondence for GL(1) is precisely the character theory of ℤ_p^×.

## Mapping to the Silent Parameter

### Automorphic Side: R(SU(2))_k

The fusion ring R(SU(2))_k is the Grothendieck ring of the modular tensor category C(SU(2), k) — the representation category of the quantum group U_q(sl₂) at q = e^{iπ/(k+2)}.

This is the **automorphic side** because:
- SU(2) is the compact real form of SL(2, ℂ)
- Representations of SU(2) are classified by Harish-Chandra parameters
- The level k truncation corresponds to restricting to the "physical" subcategory
- The fusion rules are the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition at finite level

In the Langlands framework for GL(1), the automorphic side at the Archimedean place (ℝ^×) consists of characters — 1-dimensional representations. R(SU(2))_k is richer because it's the representation theory of the **quantum group** (not just GL(1)), but the structure is analogous.

### Galois Side: R(ℤ₂^×)

The character group R(ℤ₂^×) consists of continuous homomorphisms:

```
χ: ℤ₂^× → ℂ^×
```

The group ℤ₂^× has the structure:

```
ℤ₂^× ≅ ℤ/2ℤ × (1 + 4ℤ₂) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ₂
```

where the first factor is the torsion subgroup (generated by -1) and the second factor is the free ℤ₂-module. The continuous characters are:

```
χ(a) = ε(a) · (a)^{s-1}
```

where ε is a character of the torsion part and s ∈ ℂ parametrizes the continuous complex characters of the free part.

The **character ring** R(ℤ₂^×) is the ring of continuous characters with convolution product. It is the local Langlands "Galois side" because ℤ₂^× is the multiplicative group of the local field ℚ₂ — the automorphic side of local class field theory. **Wait — this is a subtle point.**

## The Crucial Subtlety

In the **standard** local Langlands for GL(1)/ℚ₂:

| Side | Standard Object |
|:-----|:----------------|
| Galois | Characters of Gal(ℚ̄₂/ℚ₂)^{ab} ≅ ℚ₂^× |
| Automorphic | Characters of ℚ₂^× |

The reciprocity map sends Galois characters to automorphic characters. Both sides are essentially the same group (local class field theory identifies W_{ℚ₂}^{ab} ≅ ℚ₂^×).

In the Silent Parameter:
- R(ℤ₂^×) is the character ring of the **automorphic** side (characters of ℤ₂^× ⊂ ℚ₂^×)
- R(SU(2))_k is the fusion ring of the **Archimedean** automorphic side (representations of SU(2) at level k)

So what's the Galois side? **It's implicit in the isomorphism itself.** The fusion ring R(SU(2))_k encodes the automorphic side at the ∞-place. The character ring R(ℤ₂^×) encodes the automorphic side at the 2-place. Their isomorphism R(SU(2))_k ≅ R(ℤ₂^×) is a **place-crossing automorphic duality** — it relates the automorphic side at different completions.

The Langlands correspondence proper relates **automorphic** to **Galois**. But the Silent Parameter relates **automorphic at ∞** to **automorphic at 2**. This is not just Langlands — it's **functoriality** (lifting between different groups/places) expressed as a fusion ring isomorphism.

## Revised Identification

The Silent Parameter R(SU(2))_k ≅ R(ℤ₂^×) is better understood as:

1. **Local Langlands for GL(1) at p = 2, restricted to the tame part:** Characters of ℤ₂^× are the tame automorphic characters. The FROBENIUS eigenvalue (the Langlands parameter at p=2) is encoded in the torsion character ε, and the continuous parameter s is the Hecke eigenvalue.

2. **Archimedean-p-adic functoriality:** The isomorphism between the fusion ring at the ∞-place (R(SU(2))_k) and the character ring at the 2-place (R(ℤ₂^×)) is a form of Langlands functoriality — a lift from the automorphic side at one place to the automorphic side at another.

3. **The Galois side is encoded in the ZBW correlator O_ZBW.** O_ZBW is the ℤ₂-valued topological invariant that distinguishes Dirac (O_ZBW = 1) from Majorana (O_ZBW = 0). As a ℤ₂ grading on the fusion ring, it is the **Galois representation** — the 1-dimensional representation of Gal(ℚ̄₂/ℚ₂) factoring through the quadratic character.
